01 / About

From logic gates to living game worlds.

I'm an Electronics & Communication Engineering student (2028 batch). My goal is to create game systems in both hardware and software — the silicon and boards that games run on, and the engines, mechanics and AI that make them fun.

On the hardware side I work in digital VLSI: RTL design, verification, and the Cadence flow from synthesis to a DRC-clean layout.

My favourite problems sit where hardware meets security: scan chains that leak secrets, ciphers that need to fit in silicon, boards that have to route cleanly on two layers.

On the software side I've spent two years building games in Unreal Engine 5 and Unity. Right now I'm using reinforcement learning to make enemies adapt to the player instead of repeating the same moves.

04

Air Mouse — 3-DoF Motion Input

PCB design · KiCad · Toward AR / VR pointing

Point at the world, not the desk. A wireless, battery-powered handheld that turns hand motion into input. Its 6-axis IMU (accelerometer + gyroscope) is the same sensor class that 3-DoF VR pointers like the Daydream controller used for rotation tracking. A teammate drew the schematic; I did the full layout — placement, hand routing, and a guide to every route.

  • LSM6DSL 6-axis IMU (accel + gyro) for 3-DoF rotation tracking
  • ESP32-S3-MINI-1 with on-board antenna (Wi-Fi + Bluetooth LE) for wireless input
  • Left / right / middle buttons for select, grab and menu actions
  • USB-C, MCP73831 Li-ion charger and AP2112K 3.3 V LDO for untethered use
  • Almost unbroken B.Cu GND plane, antenna keep-out, Gerbers ready
  • Scope today: PCB hardware only — rotation only (no positional tracking), no magnetometer, firmware not yet written

ValoSkill — Economic Performance in Tactical Shooters

Role-aware rating · VALORANT · CS:GO · CS2
Finding 01 / 04

ValoSkill: a role-aware rating

Kills and K/D measure how much a player shoots, not how well they do their job. ValoSkill scores every player on their role's tasks, their impact on winning, how efficiently they spend credits, and how they perform given the situation.

4,455VALORANT matches
1.26MKills analysed
Finding 02 / 04

Not all kills are equal

A round win-probability model credits each kill by how much it changed the odds. A kill in a 5v5 adds about 17% win chance — a 1v1 kill adds about 45%.

0.84–0.93Role model AUC
Finding 03 / 04

Force buys hurt most

An economic layer (spending threshold E*, efficiency, cost per completed task) shows half-buys at 1–2k credits perform worse than full ecos — in both PScore and team win rate.

Validated on pro Counter-Strike

Extended to 1,558 CS:GO and ~2,000 CS2 pro maps with behaviour-based roles. PScore agrees with an HLTV-style rating, and a replication of the optimal-economy study holds on modern CS2.

Ongoing research

Adaptive RL Opponents

Unity 6 · ML-Agents · PPO · Player experience

Enemies that don't repeat themselves. Scripted game AI falls into patterns players learn and exploit. This project uses RL so opponents adapt to each player and keep varying their behaviour.

Three AI race cars with distinct "personalities" — Speedster, Technician, Rocket — trained with PPO through a five-stage curriculum, then benchmarked against a classic pure-pursuit scripted bot driving the identical car.

  • 72 observations (17 vector + 11 ray-casts), 2 continuous actions every 0.1 s
  • Shaped rewards for progress, overtakes, head-to-head wins and clean driving
  • Adaptive Director tunes pace (0.70–1.12×) per player and remembers them
NowAdaptive racing opponents

RL cars that vary their lines and pace per player, so races never play out the same way twice.

NextToward GT Sophy

Extending the work toward Wurman et al., Nature 2022: mixed-scenario training and a sportsmanship-aware reward for close, fair racing.

ThenCombat enemies

Bringing the same adaptive RL to Sekiro-style melee enemies that read the player and mix up their attack patterns.
